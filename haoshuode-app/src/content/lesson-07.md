Not every adjective gets its own dedicated word. Hao-shuo-de builds "strong" out of two words you already know: <audio-example zh="有">{{word:you3}}</audio-example> ("to have," from Lesson 6) plus <audio-example zh="力量">{{word:li4liang4}}</audio-example> ("power, energy") — <audio-example zh="有力量">{{word:you3}} {{word:li4liang4}}</audio-example>, literally "to have power." Bound with `-{{word:de}}` the same way any description binds onto a noun, <audio-example zh="有力量的">{{word:you3}}-{{word:li4liang4}}-{{word:de}}</audio-example> modifies directly: <audio-example zh="有力量的男人">{{word:you3}}-{{word:li4liang4}}-{{word:de}} {{word:nan2ren2}}</audio-example> ("a strong man").

Adjectives can modify other adjectives or verbs by preceding them directly as adverbs (e.g., `{{word:hen3}} {{word:duo1}}` for "very many").

> ### The Causative Rule
>
> To turn an adjective into a transitive action (such as transforming "good" into "to fix/improve" or "bad" into "to break"), you must use the standard Mandarin {{word:ba3}} construction paired with {{word:bian4}} (to become/change):
>
> `Subject + {{word:ba3}} + Object + {{word:bian4}} + Adjective`
